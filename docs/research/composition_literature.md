# Composition literature: experts, routers, stitches, inserted layers

Date of search: 2026-11-14. Method: web search plus reading the paper HTML. Every number below came from the cited page. Items marked UNVERIFIED were not confirmed in a primary text. PDFs for BTX and BTS failed to parse, so those were read from the arXiv HTML pages.

Bottom line first: no source shows a router-only, ~1000-step recipe that beats the specialists on their own domains. The best low-training results come from (a) closed-form or data-seeded router init (DUME, UpIT, Nexus), (b) same-family experts with an alignment step (Symphony-MoE), and (c) zero-init inserted blocks trained on in-domain data (LLaMA Pro). Heterogeneous experts (MergeME) work only with full-parameter training on tens of billions of tokens.

LoRA experts versus MLP experts: no source compares them head to head under matched conditions (searched, none found). Indirect evidence: GOAT says LoRA-MoE often trails full fine-tuning because rank is split across experts (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16894v1); UpIT runs both and reports gains over its own baseline in each but does not isolate the gap (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01610).

Cross-architecture transplant: I found no source that moves real decoder layers or expert MLPs between different families at fixed hidden size and trains only the seams. Closest are MergeME (projectors, 40B tokens) and HeteroFusion (adapter space). Transport and Merge (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05495) uses optimal transport on activations; I did not read it, so its numbers are UNVERIFIED.

## 4. Negative results collected

- Naive concatenation of misaligned same-family experts is catastrophic (33.94 vs 44.07), https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18542v1.
- Dense merge of experts without training loses to the seed (2.8B: 43.3 vs 47.1), https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15901v1.
- Training-free routing on heterogeneous experts misranks the specialist (math expert second on GSM8K and MATH), https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00997v3.
- Frozen specialist stacks with only stitches train no new capability: BTS without in-domain data equals the seed, https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00075v1.
- Experts trained too long merge worse, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14126v1.
- Depth removal collapses knowledge QA at a model-specific threshold, Qwen lowest, https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17887v2.
- Self-MoE style joint expert and router training on little data collapsed with FFN experts, https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01610.
- Specialists keep their edge: no composed MoE here beats the code specialist on HumanEval (Symphony) or the math specialist on math (DUME 91.1 vs 100).

## Settings to use

Design 1: routed MLP experts inside the base (same-family Qwen2.5 specialists first)

1. Init the routers in closed form, not random. Use DUME's ridge fit, lambda 0.01, on a few hundred to few thousand tokens per specialist domain, per layer (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29765). UpIT shows seeded router init is the largest single factor (52.21 vs 49.96 for none, 49.30 for random-data seeding) (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.01610). Only random-init routers appear in Symphony and Llama-3 recipes, so expect them to need more steps.
2. Keep the base MLP as an expert (or an always-on shared expert as in Nexus, https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15901v1). Every successful recipe has a generalist anchor. Use top-2 over base plus specialists, softmax over all logits then top-k (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07524v2). Do not use top-1 for the first run: top-1 switch routing was worst in BTX.
3. Experts and non-MLP weights frozen, router trained, then optionally LoRA. Symphony and BTX both show frozen expert FFNs lose nothing (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18542v1, https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07816). Router LR 1e-5 (DUME+, 1000 iterations) to 5e-5 (Symphony). Start at 5e-5 for 1000 to 1500 steps; 1e-5 is the fallback if gates oscillate.
4. Load-balance weight 0.01 (BTX, Symphony, NVIDIA study). Do not drop it to zero: BTX saw HumanEval fall 27.4 to 19.5, and the NVIDIA study saw dead experts. With only three experts, consider 1e-3 so it does not fight genuine domain skew (Nexus saw 0.0005 hurt a plain router but not a domain-informed one).
5. Data mixture follows BTX: include about 20% generalist data in the mix, and weight each specialist domain by its share of use. Our tiny budget means data must hit each specialist's domain; BTS and Symphony both show gains vanish without in-domain data.
6. Same-family only works if neurons are aligned. Qwen2.5-Instruct, Coder and Math were all fine-tuned from related bases, so alignment is likely good, but check by measuring CKA or per-layer cosine of MLP activations; run the Hungarian permutation from Symphony if the specialists were not tuned from the same checkpoint (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.18542v1). Qwen2.5-Coder had extra code-heavy continued pretraining (UNVERIFIED here), so it is the highest risk.
7. Do not train specialists or base long when you produce adapters (Less is More): peak merge quality arrives early (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14126v1). Relevant only if we retrain any specialist.
8. Cross-family experts behind linear stitches: expect to need far more than 1500 steps. MergeME needed 40B tokens of full fine-tuning and still misranked the specialist (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00997v3). Initialize each Proj-in and Proj-out from a least-squares fit of the donor hidden state to the base hidden state on calibration text instead of random (our proposal, not from a source). Keep the sequence-level perplexity router (MergeME) as a zero-training fallback.
9. Baselines to report: base alone, each specialist, DARE or TIES merge of the three (DUME, MergeME show averaging loses 10 to 25 points of normalized score), and DUME init without training. Expected realistic outcome from the literature: 90 to 97% of each specialist on its domain, not a win over it.

Design 2: specialist decoder layers inserted into the base stack

1. Zero-init the output projections of each inserted block (attention O and MLP down) and put the gate at zero; do not zero the norm scale (https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02415). Add the in-stitch and out-stitch as identity-initialized linear maps when hidden sizes match, and least-squares-initialized maps when they differ (the latter is our proposal).
2. Insert in the middle band, interleaved one block per group, never at the bottom, and few at a time: LLaMA Pro interleaved best, bottom worst; Painters shows early layers are not swappable; NOT found only 1 to 2 organs compose (https://arxiv.org/html/2401.02415, https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09298v3, https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13580v1). Start with 1 to 2 donor layers, not many.
3. Use same-family donors first. Evidence for cross-architecture layer insertion is absent (NOT lists it as unexplored). Treat any cross-family insertion as an experiment with no literature prior.
4. Training must include the donor domain. LLaMA Pro trained ~80B tokens of in-domain data on new blocks; at our 1000 to 1500 steps, expect a small gain and pick eval metrics matched to the donor. Your side-branch failure with seam-only training matches BTS, where gains need stitches at several depths plus in-domain data (4 stitches vs 1: 28.1 vs 27.0) (https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00075v1).
5. LoRA on base and donor layers, FFN-focused, per the healing recipe: rank 64 default (lower for 7B and larger), LR 3e-4 with cosine and 100 warmup, but reduce to 3e-5 to 3e-6 if the model is fragile (https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17887v2). Our batch of 8 is below their batch of 16 and their 5000 steps, so expect partial recovery only. LoRA only on boundary layers is a claim from a NeurIPS 2026 listing that I could not verify.
6. Prefer stacking that adds depth (insert, as in SOLAR and LLaMA Pro) over removing depth. Carving to half depth is near or past the collapse threshold for Qwen (about 20% of layers removable before MMLU falls apart in Gromov et al.). If depth must shrink, cap near 15 to 20% for Qwen and heal on generation loss, not on knowledge QA; GSM8K degrades immediately with any removal.
7. SOLAR-style duplication is not evidence for our case: it needed full continued pretraining and its token budget is unreported (https://arxiv.org/html/2312.15166v2). Goliath-style interleaving has no benchmarks (https://huggingface.co/alpindale/goliath-120b/discussions/3).

Pitfalls across both

- No source reports a composed model beating its specialist on the specialist's own benchmark with a small budget; plan to claim parity-in-domain plus better generalism than a merge.
- Measure per-layer routing entropy and expert share early; the failures that were diagnosed (dead experts, specialist misrank) show up there before accuracy moves.
- Several results above come from 1B to 3B models with small benchmark averages; differences under about 1 point are within noise (Self-MoE reports a standard deviation of about 0.6, https://arxiv.org/html/2406.12034v2).
